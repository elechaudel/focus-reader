focus-reader

A text read a few words at a time, like subtitles, in a panel with its pace, size and progress.

Most of what we read on a screen asks the eye to do two jobs at once: follow the words, and find its way back to the line after every glance away. The focus reader takes the second job off its hands. It shows the text a few words at a time, in the same place, at a pace you choose, and you only have to read.

It has two views. Sequence puts each frame on a band between two rulers, one word or a few at a time. Flow shows the whole text and moves it up a line at a time, the words being read marked, the way an app shows the lyrics of a song. In Flow, a frame can also be a whole paragraph, with a line in the margin that fills as its time goes.

Reading back matters more than reading fast. Pause, and the sentence shows under the band; the arrows, a scroll or a swipe go a frame back or on. A comma makes a frame stay a beat longer, the end of a sentence two, and the whole is scaled so the rate shown is the rate you get.

It is a single custom element, with no dependency and no framework. Its colours and fonts come from custom properties, its words from a labels attribute, so it fits a page and speaks its language.

In the page

Without modal, the reader sits in the page and fills its box. This one starts in Flow and reads its own text, in French, with French labels.

Styled by the page

The same element, its colours and typeface set from the page with --fr-* custom properties, in light and dark.

On your page

<button onclick="document.querySelector('focus-reader').open()">Focus</button>
<focus-reader for="article-body" heading="A title" modal persist></focus-reader>
